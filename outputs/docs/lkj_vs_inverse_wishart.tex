\section*{Motivation}

\texttt{build\_design\_matrix} draws all non-context main-effect regressors
jointly from a shared latent multivariate normal, $X \sim \mathcal N(0, \Sigma)$,
so that the design can exhibit realistic collinearity instead of always being
orthogonal. Historically $\Sigma$ was drawn as $\Sigma \sim \IW(\nu, I_k)$ and
then rescaled to unit variance per column, i.e. only the \emph{correlation
matrix} implied by $\Sigma$ was ever used. This note works out exactly what
distribution that implied correlation matrix has, and shows it is the LKJ
distribution only in the bivariate case $k=2$ --- for $k>2$ (the regime this
simulator actually samples, e.g.\ \texttt{r4c4}) the two are genuinely
different distributions, which is why the swap to sampling $R \sim \LKJ(\eta)$
directly is a change in behavior, not merely a reparametrization.

\section*{Setup}

For a $k\times k$ correlation matrix $R$ (symmetric, unit diagonal, positive
definite), the LKJ distribution has density
\[
  p_{\LKJ}(R \mid \eta) \;\propto\; \det(R)^{\eta - 1}, \qquad \eta > 0.
\]
$\eta = 1$ is uniform over the space of valid correlation matrices; $\eta > 1$
concentrates mass toward the identity (weak correlations); $\eta < 1$ spreads
mass toward near-singular/extreme correlations.

The inverse-Wishart distribution on a covariance matrix $\Sigma$ with scale
$I_k$ and degrees of freedom $\nu$ has density
\[
  p_{\IW}(\Sigma \mid \nu, I_k) \;\propto\;
  \det(\Sigma)^{-(\nu+k+1)/2} \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1})\right).
\]

To compare the two, decompose $\Sigma$ into scale and correlation parts
(the ``separation strategy,'' Barnard, McCulloch \& Meng, 2000):
\[
  \Sigma = D R D, \qquad D = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k),
\]
where $\sigma_i > 0$ are standard deviations and $R$ is a correlation matrix.
This is exactly the object \texttt{build\_design\_matrix} extracts when it
computes $X_{\mathrm{mvn}} \mathrel{/}= \sqrt{\operatorname{diag}(\Sigma)}$: it
discards $D$ and keeps only $R$.

\subsection*{Change-of-variables Jacobian}

Writing $\Sigma_{ii} = \sigma_i^2$ and $\Sigma_{ij} = \sigma_i \sigma_j r_{ij}$
for $i<j$, the Jacobian matrix of $(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k, \{r_{ij}\}) \mapsto
\{\Sigma_{ij}\}$ is block lower-triangular: each $\Sigma_{ii}$ depends only on
$\sigma_i$, and each $\Sigma_{ij}$ depends on $\sigma_i,\sigma_j$ and its own
$r_{ij}$ only. The two diagonal blocks give determinant $\prod_i 2\sigma_i$
(from $\partial \Sigma_{ii}/\partial \sigma_i = 2\sigma_i$) and
$\prod_{i<j}\sigma_i\sigma_j = |D|^{k-1}$ (each index appears in $k-1$ pairs),
so
\[
  d\Sigma \;=\; 2^k\,|D|\cdot|D|^{k-1}\, dD\, dR \;=\; 2^k\,|D|^{k}\, dD\, dR.
\]
\emph{Sanity check} ($k=1$): $\Sigma=\sigma^2 \Rightarrow d\Sigma = 2\sigma\,d\sigma$,
matching $2^1|D|^1$ with no $R$ factor.

\section*{The distribution of the extracted correlation matrix}

Substituting $\Sigma = DRD$, $\det(\Sigma) = |D|^2\det(R)$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}) = \sum_i (R^{-1})_{ii}/\sigma_i^2$ (since
$\Sigma^{-1} = D^{-1}R^{-1}D^{-1}$ and $D$ is diagonal) into $p_\IW$, then
multiplying by the Jacobian:
\[
  p(D, R) \;\propto\; \det(R)^{-(\nu+k+1)/2}
  \prod_{i=1}^k \Big[ \sigma_i^{-(\nu+1)} \exp\!\big(-\tfrac{(R^{-1})_{ii}}{2\sigma_i^2}\big) \Big].
\]
The $D$-dependence factors completely across coordinates, so each $\sigma_i$
integrates out independently:
\[
  \int_0^\infty \sigma^{-(\nu+1)} \exp\!\left(-\frac{c}{2\sigma^2}\right) d\sigma
  \;\propto\; c^{-\nu/2}, \qquad c = (R^{-1})_{ii}.
\]
Marginalizing $D$ out therefore gives the \textbf{exact} density of the
correlation matrix implied by $\Sigma \sim \IW(\nu, I_k)$:
\[
  \boxed{\; p_R(R) \;\propto\; \det(R)^{-(\nu+k+1)/2} \prod_{i=1}^k \big[(R^{-1})_{ii}\big]^{-\nu/2} \;}
\]

\section*{$k=2$: exact equivalence}

For $k=2$, $R = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & r \\ r & 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$\det(R) = 1-r^2$, and $(R^{-1})_{11} = (R^{-1})_{22} = (1-r^2)^{-1}$ --- both
diagonal entries of $R^{-1}$ are \emph{pure functions of $\det(R)$}. Substituting,
\[
  p_R(r) \;\propto\; (1-r^2)^{-(\nu+3)/2} \cdot (1-r^2)^{\nu/2} \cdot (1-r^2)^{\nu/2}
  \;=\; (1-r^2)^{(\nu-3)/2}.
\]
Compared with the LKJ density in two dimensions, $p_{\LKJ}(r) \propto (1-r^2)^{\eta-1}$,
these match exactly under
\[
  \eta = \frac{\nu - 1}{2} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \nu = 2\eta + 1.
\]
So extracting the correlation from a $2\times 2$ inverse-Wishart$(\nu, I_2)$
draw and sampling $r \sim \LKJ(\eta)$ directly are \emph{literally the same
distribution} under this correspondence.

\paragraph{Numerical check.} With $\eta=2 \Rightarrow \nu=5$, $N=\num{200000}$
draws each: IW-extracted $r$ has mean $-0.0015$, std $0.4463$; direct LKJ draws
have mean $0.0005$, std $0.4470$. A two-sample KS test gives
$D_{\mathrm{KS}}=0.0034$, $p=0.20$ --- indistinguishable, as the algebra predicts.

\section*{$k>2$: the equivalence breaks down}

For $k>2$, $(R^{-1})_{ii}$ is \emph{not} a function of $\det(R)$ alone --- it
depends on the full correlation structure among all $k$ variables, not just
their overall determinant. So $\prod_i[(R^{-1})_{ii}]^{-\nu/2}$ cannot be
absorbed into the $\det(R)^{-(\nu+k+1)/2}$ term, and the boxed density above
does not reduce to LKJ's $\det(R)^{\eta-1}$ form for any $\nu$. The two
families are genuinely different distributions once $k>2$.

\paragraph{Numerical check ($k=4$, $\eta=2$).} Naively extending the $k=2$
correspondence ($\nu = 2\eta+1 = 5$), the single pairwise entry $r_{12}$
extracted from $\Sigma \sim \IW(5, I_4)$ has std $0.5768$, versus the true LKJ$(2)$
pairwise marginal (which is $2\cdot\mathrm{Beta}(\eta+\tfrac{k-2}{2},\,\eta+\tfrac{k-2}{2})-1$
by exchangeability, std $0.3781$). A KS test rejects equality outright
($D_{\mathrm{KS}}=0.147$, $p\approx 0$): IW-then-normalize produces
\emph{substantially more extreme} pairwise correlations than a matched-$\eta$
LKJ draw once $k>2$.

\section*{Why the old code needed a $k$-dependent range}

This also explains a detail of the previous implementation: the degrees of
freedom were drawn as $\nu \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(k+1,\, 4k+1)$, scaling with
$k$. Because IW's implied pairwise-correlation spread drifts with $k$ for
fixed $\nu$ (as the table above shows: same $\nu$, very different spread at
$k=2$ vs.\ $k=4$), that $k$-dependent range was an empirically-tuned patch to
keep the \emph{effective} collinearity roughly comparable across different
regressor counts. LKJ's $\eta$ does not need this patch --- by construction,
every pairwise marginal of an LKJ$(\eta)$ draw has the same
$2\cdot\mathrm{Beta}(\eta+\tfrac{k-2}{2},\,\eta+\tfrac{k-2}{2})-1$ form, so a
single $\eta$-sampling range behaves consistently as $k$ varies (up to the
principled, derived $\tfrac{k-2}{2}$ shift, not an ad hoc one).

\section*{Bottom line}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Extracting a correlation matrix from $\Sigma \sim \IW(\nu, I_k)$ and
    sampling $R \sim \LKJ(\eta)$ directly are \textbf{exactly the same
    distribution only when $k=2$}, under $\nu = 2\eta+1$.
  \item For $k>2$ --- the regime this simulator actually uses --- they are
    different distributions; IW-then-normalize is not a valid way to sample
    from LKJ$(\eta)$ in more than two dimensions.
  \item The swap implemented in \texttt{design\_utils.build\_design\_matrix}
    (using \texttt{sample\_lkj\_correlation} from
    \texttt{simulator\_utils.py}) is therefore a genuine change in the
    training data's collinearity structure for $k>2$, not just a faster
    or cleaner-looking reparametrization of the same thing.
\end{itemize}
